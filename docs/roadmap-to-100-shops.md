# InkTracker — Road to 100 Shops

A milestone plan to grow from ~10 paying shops to 100, built for a bootstrapped, solo, printer-founder with a niche $99/mo product. Milestone-based, not date-locked — pace scales with the hours you put in. Last updated: 2026-07.

---

## The north star (what 100 shops means)

- **100 shops × $99/mo = $9,900 MRR ≈ $119k ARR.** Enough to be a real business and to justify going full-time if you aren't already.
- Annual plan ($999/yr) pulls cash forward and cuts churn — push it once you have proof.
- **The whole game is two numbers:** shops added per month, and shops lost per month (churn). At 100 shops, even 3%/mo churn = ~3 lost every month, so retention becomes as important as acquisition. Build for both from the start.

### Funnel math (so the targets are grounded)
Assumptions for a well-onboarded, founder-touched niche tool:
- Trial → paid conversion: **30–40%** early (founder onboards each), settling to ~25–30% as it self-serves.
- Monthly churn: keep under **3–4%**. Under 3% is where compounding really works.
- So to *net* +5 shops/month you need roughly **15–20 trials/month** landing, plus holding churn low.

| To grow at… | Trials/mo needed (~33% conv) | Net adds/mo (after ~3% churn) |
|---|---|---|
| Early (10–25) | 8–12 | +3–5 |
| Middle (25–50) | 15–20 | +5–8 |
| Later (50–100) | 25–35 | +8–12 |

## The milestone chart

| Stage | Shops | Theme | Primary motion | Realistic window* | Exit criteria (don't advance until…) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Foundation** | ~10 | Product + proof are solid | Harden, instrument, get listed | done / now | Product stable, backups/DR done, blog + /compare live, directories submitted |
| **1 — Founder-led** | 10 → 25 | Hand-to-hand, first proof | 1:1 outreach + community + reviews | ~2–4 mo | 5+ public reviews, 2 case studies/testimonials, onboarding that converts, churn measured |
| **2 — Repeatable** | 25 → 50 | Find 2 channels that work | SEO/directories compounding + PLG loop + referrals | ~4–8 mo | 2 channels with known CAC, a working referral, churn <4% |
| **3 — Scale winners** | 50 → 75 | Pour fuel on what works | Content engine + directory PPC + partnerships | ~4–7 mo | Onboarding mostly self-serve, first partnership live, annual plan adopted |
| **4 — Systematize** | 75 → 100 | Flywheel + retention | Optimize channels, expansion, word-of-mouth | ~4–6 mo | 100 shops, <3% churn, predictable pipeline |

*Windows assume steady part-to-full-time effort. Total: roughly **14–24 months** at a sustainable solo pace. Going harder compresses it; churn drags it out.

## Plan of action, by stage

### Stage 1 — Founder-led (10 → 25)
This is the stage where you win by hand. Nobody scales past this without doing unscalable things first.

- **10–15 founder outreaches per week.** Personal notes to shops you know or admire: "I'm a printer, built the tool I wanted, I'll set you up myself and you keep it free through the trial." The first ~50 customers of a niche B2B tool come from the founder doing this by hand.
- **Community presence, authentically.** Post as a builder-printer in the big Facebook screen-printing groups and r/screenprinting — show a real screenshot, ask for feedback, don't pitch. Printers reward builders and punish ad-speak.
- **Turn on the directory + review engine (see "How to list" below).** Get the G2/Capterra listing live and start collecting reviews — this is the compounding foundation.
- **Nail onboarding.** You have an onboarding wizard and an (unshipped) onboarding assistant. Personally onboard every trial for now, and watch where they stall — that's your conversion leak. Ship the assistant once the flow is proven.
- **Manufacture proof.** Get 5+ honest reviews and 2 short case studies ("switched from spreadsheets/Printavo, here's what changed"). Proof is what makes every later channel work.
- **Ship the PLG wizard mark** (docs/growth-wizard-loop.md): a tasteful "made with InkTracker" footer on public quote/approval/wizard pages. Every quote your shops send becomes a billboard to other printers. Costs nothing, compounds forever.

### Stage 2 — Repeatable acquisition (25 → 50)
Stop doing everything; find the 1–2 channels with the best cost-per-shop and lean in.

- **SEO/content cadence** (docs/blog-strategy.md): 1 evergreen cornerstone post every 2 weeks + a light weekly "Print Shop Brief." Publish the pricing, tax, embroidery, and honest-comparison posts already drafted. These pull in printers actively shopping, for years.
- **Directory reviews → ranking.** Keep the review flywheel turning; a listing with a cluster of reviews starts ranking for "best screen printing software" and feeds the listicles that currently outrank you.
- **Referral program.** A dead-simple "give a month, get a month" — printers talk to each other constantly. Track referrals explicitly.
- **First partnership.** One blank-supplier newsletter, one popular screen-printing YouTuber, or one big FB group admin. A single well-placed mention can outperform months of cold outreach.
- **Measure CAC + channel attribution.** Tag every CTA (`?ref=`) so you know which channel actually produces paying shops. Kill what doesn't work.

### Stage 3 — Scale the winners (50 → 75)
- **Content engine**: keep the cadence; refresh top posts; expand the /compare cluster.
- **Directory PPC** — *now* that you have reviews, paid placement on Capterra/G2 sends traffic to a profile that converts (never before reviews exist).
- **Onboarding self-serve**: automate what you were doing by hand (guided setup, sample data, the onboarding assistant, short Loom walkthroughs) so signups don't need you.
- **Push annual billing** ($999/yr) to pull cash forward and lock in retention.
- **Formalize 2–3 partnerships** with clear referral tracking.

### Stage 4 — Systematize + retain (75 → 100)
- **Retention is now a growth lever.** Watch feature adoption; reach out to shops that go quiet; the read-only-on-expiry work you built protects lapsed accounts' data so they can come back.
- **Word-of-mouth flywheel**: reviews + PLG mark + referrals compound. Most of your late-stage growth should be shops hearing about you from other shops.
- **Expansion revenue**: an eventual add-on tier (e.g., white-label the customer quote pages — the natural upsell once you have tiers) lifts revenue without new logos.
- **Predictable pipeline**: you should be able to forecast next month's adds within a few shops.

## Channels, ranked for a bootstrapped printer tool

1. **Founder-led outreach + community** — highest early ROI, zero cost, builds trust. Start here.
2. **Product-led wizard loop** — every quote is an ad to your exact audience. Compounds with every active shop. Free.
3. **SEO: blog + /compare + directories** — pulls in printers who are actively shopping. Slow to start, compounds for years.
4. **Reviews (G2/Capterra)** — the trust layer that makes 3 and 5 work.
5. **Referrals** — printers trust other printers more than any ad.
6. **Partnerships** — suppliers, YouTubers, FB groups; one good one is worth months of cold work.
7. **Directory PPC / light paid ads** — only *after* reviews and a converting funnel exist. Never pay to send traffic to an empty profile.

Do **not** try to run all seven at once. Add channels as you graduate stages.

## How to list (the directory + review playbook)

Getting listed is step one; **reviews are the actual lever** — a listing with no reviews doesn't rank or convert.

### The listings (in priority order)
1. **G2 Digital Markets — one submission covers Capterra + GetApp + Software Advice.** Since G2 acquired all three, they share one vendor system. *Status: your listing is built and sitting on the "ready to review" screen — submit it, then it's in moderation (days to weeks) before going live.* After approval, extend it to GetApp and Software Advice from the same dashboard (a placement request, not a new signup).
2. **G2 profile** — if it converges further, one presence; today, claim/confirm the G2 profile too.
3. **Your own owned surfaces** — the `/compare` buyer's guide and the blog *are* your highest-intent "listings," and you control them. Keep them fair and current.
4. **Niche + adjacent directories** — Product Hunt (a launch spike + backlinks), relevant print-industry directories, Google Business Profile, and any blank-supplier or trade-org marketplaces.

### The review engine (do this the day each listing goes live)
- Use each platform's **official review-invite link** from the vendor dashboard (verified flow).
- Email your **10–15 happiest shops** with a short, personal ask.
- **Never** incentivize, write, or staff-post reviews — G2/Capterra detect and penalize it. Real users only.
- Aim for the small cluster (a handful) each platform needs to show a public star rating — that first cluster flips a listing from a stub into a lead source.
- Keep it going: make "leave a review" a gentle step after a shop's first successful month.

### What's already built you can lean on
- Audited, hardened product; data durability + DR work in flight.
- Static, crawlable blog with Article + FAQ schema; the `/compare` buyer's guide; `/for-printers` PLG landing page.
- Directory listing kit (docs/directory-listing-kit.md) and the G2 listing itself.
- The wizard-loop PLG plan (docs/growth-wizard-loop.md) and blog strategy (docs/blog-strategy.md).

## Retention (protect the base you're building)

Acquisition is wasted if the bucket leaks. At every stage:
- Track monthly churn and *why* shops leave. Target <3–4%.
- Onboarding is the #1 retention lever — a shop that gets to its first successful quote/order stays.
- Data durability + read-only-on-expiry (both built) mean lapsed shops don't lose data and can reactivate — a quiet retention win.
- A quarterly "here's what's new" note keeps you top-of-mind.

## Solo-founder operating rhythm (so this survives you)

- **Weekly:** 10–15 outreaches, 1 community post, respond to every trial personally, 1 Brief.
- **Biweekly:** 1 cornerstone blog post.
- **Monthly:** review the metrics dashboard, pick the next channel bet, ask happy shops for reviews.
- **Batch content**; don't promise weekly essays (burnout). Founder-voice in, AI-polish out — printers smell generic filler.

## The dashboard to watch (5 numbers)
1. Paying shops (and MRR)
2. Trials started / month
3. Trial → paid conversion %
4. Monthly churn %
5. Signups attributed per channel (`?ref=` tags)

If those five trend right, 100 shops is a matter of time.
