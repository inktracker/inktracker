# Stage 1 — The First 30 Days (10 → toward 25)

The founder-led sprint. You win this stage by hand: talking to printers one at a time, getting your first public proof, and turning on the loops that compound later. Everything here is copy-paste-ready — swap the [brackets] and send.

## What "done" looks like in 30 days
- **+5–8 trials started**, personally onboarded, tracked.
- **5+ honest public reviews** live on your directory listing.
- **2 short case studies / testimonials** from real shops.
- **G2/Capterra listing submitted** and the review-invite emails sent.
- **"Made with InkTracker" mark shipped** on public quote/approval pages.
- A simple tracking sheet you actually update.

## Voice rules (this is what makes it work)
You're a printer who built the thing, not a software company. Every message should sound like it came from a person at a press, because it did.
- First person, plain, specific. Short is fine.
- Lead with *you built it for your own shop*, not with features.
- Ask for feedback, not the sale. Printers reward builders and smell ad-speak instantly.
- No "revolutionize," "seamless," "game-changer," "excited to announce." If it sounds like a press release, rewrite it.

## Week 1 — Turn on the foundation

- [ ] **Submit the G2/Capterra listing** (it's built and waiting). Moderation takes days–weeks; get the clock started.
- [ ] **Ship the "Made with InkTracker" footer mark** on QuotePayment, ArtApproval, and the public wizard (docs/growth-wizard-loop.md). Every quote your shops send now advertises you.
- [ ] **Build your list of 30 shops** to reach out to: people you know, shops you admire, printers whose work you follow. Split into "know personally" and "admire from afar."
- [ ] **Set up a one-tab tracking sheet:** Shop | Contact | Channel | Status (reached / replied / trial / paying / passed) | Notes | ref-tag.
- [ ] **Send your first 10 founder outreaches** (templates below). Start with the ones you know.

## Week 2 — Outreach + community

- [ ] **Send 10–15 more outreaches.** Warm ones first, then the admire list.
- [ ] **Post one authentic build story** in the biggest screen-printing Facebook group and r/screenprinting (template below). Reply to every comment.
- [ ] **Onboard every trial personally** — hop on a 15-min call or Loom them a walkthrough. Watch where they get stuck; that stall point is your #1 conversion leak. Write it down.
- [ ] **Follow up** with anyone who read but didn't reply (template below).

## Week 3 — Convert + collect proof

- [ ] **Keep outreach at 10–15/week.** It's a rhythm, not a burst.
- [ ] **Nudge trials toward their first real quote/order** — that first success is what converts and retains.
- [ ] **Once the listing is live, send review-invite emails** to your happiest 10–15 shops (template below). Use the platform's official invite link only.
- [ ] **Ask your 2 best shops for a testimonial / mini case study** (template below).

## Week 4 — Tighten + repeat

- [ ] **Second community post** — this time share something useful (a pricing tip, a workflow you fixed), mention the tool lightly at the end.
- [ ] **Fix the #1 onboarding stall** you found in weeks 2–3. Even a small fix lifts every future conversion.
- [ ] **Review the numbers:** trials started, trial→paid, where signups came from. Double down on whatever channel actually produced a paying shop.
- [ ] **Line up next month:** refill the outreach list to 30, draft two more community posts.

## Templates

### 1. Founder outreach — a shop you know
> Subject: built some print shop software — want a look?
>
> Hey [Name] — [how you know them / "we met at [event]" / "been following [shop]'s work for a while"].
>
> I got sick of running quotes and production out of spreadsheets and QuickBooks double-entry, so I built the tool I wanted for my own shop. It does quoting with live blank pricing, a production board, and a real two-way QuickBooks sync — one flat price, everything included.
>
> I'm setting up the first shops myself. Want me to get you going? You keep it free through the trial, no card. Even if it's not for you, I'd take the honest feedback.
>
> — Joe

### 2. Founder outreach — a shop you admire (colder)
> Subject: a printer built you some software
>
> Hi [Name] — I run a shop and I've admired [shop]'s [specific thing — their sim-process work / their turnaround / their brand] for a while.
>
> Quick reason I'm reaching out: I built print-shop software for my own shop — quotes, production, invoicing, QuickBooks — because nothing out there fit how we actually work. I'm not a software company, just a printer who got tired of the tools.
>
> I'll set you up personally and you run it free through the trial. Worth a look? Happy to just send a 2-minute video if that's easier.
>
> — Joe, [your shop]

### 3. Follow-up (no reply after ~4–5 days)
> Hey [Name] — floating this back up in case it got buried. No pressure at all. If quoting/production out of spreadsheets is a pain point, I'd love to set you up; if it's not, no worries. Either way, happy to answer anything.

### 4. Community launch post (Facebook group / r/screenprinting)
> **I'm a printer and I got fed up with our tools, so I built our own. Sharing it + would love your feedback.**
>
> Running our shop out of spreadsheets and re-typing everything into QuickBooks was killing me, and the software I tried never fit how a print shop actually works. So over the last while I built [InkTracker] — quoting with live blank pricing from S&S and AS Colour, a production board the whole shop can see, invoicing that actually syncs both ways with QuickBooks, and an order form customers can use on our own site.
>
> It's one flat price, everything included, built by someone who's actually pulling prints.
>
> I'm not here to hard-sell — I'd genuinely love feedback from other shops on what's missing or what you'd want. Screenshot below. Ask me anything, and if you want to kick the tires I'll set you up myself.

*(Post as yourself, not a brand account. Include one real screenshot. Reply to every comment. Follow each group's self-promo rules — some want you to ask a mod first.)*

### 5. Value-first community post (week 4 — softer)
> **How I finally stopped losing money on setup fees (pricing lesson that cost me a season)**
>
> [Share the real margin-vs-markup lesson from the pricing blog — the "cost times 1.5 isn't a 50% margin" story. Teach it straight, no pitch.]
>
> I ended up baking this logic into the software I built for our shop, but honestly the lesson matters whether you use any tool or a napkin. Happy to share how I set our price chart up if it's useful.

### 6. Review request (after a shop's had a good first month)
> Hey [Name] — really glad [InkTracker]'s been working for [shop]. Quick favor: we just got listed on [Capterra/G2] and a couple of honest reviews would mean a lot for helping other printers find it. Here's the link — takes a few minutes: [official invite link]. Totally fine if not, and thank you either way.

*(Never offer discounts or anything for a review — the platforms detect it and it can get your listing penalized. Real, unincentivized reviews only.)*

### 7. Case study / testimonial ask
> Hey [Name] — would you be up for a couple sentences on what switching to [InkTracker] changed for [shop]? Even just "we used to do X, now we do Y" is perfect. I'd use it on the site (with your shop name + a link back to you, if you're good with that). And if you'd ever do a quick screenshot of your board, even better. No worries if you'd rather not.

## The one-tab tracker (keep it stupid-simple)
| Shop | Contact | Channel | Status | ref-tag | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [shop] | [name/email] | DM / FB / referral | reached → replied → trial → paying / passed | ?ref=dm | where they stalled, follow-up date |

Update it after every message. At month's end, the "Channel" + "Status" columns tell you what's actually working — that's how you decide where Stage 2 goes.

## Daily rhythm (30–60 min is enough)
- **Mon–Fri:** 3–4 outreaches or follow-ups; reply to every trial/comment same day.
- **Twice this month:** one community post.
- **Once a week:** update the tracker, look at what converted.

Do this for 30 days and Stage 1 basically runs itself into Stage 2.
